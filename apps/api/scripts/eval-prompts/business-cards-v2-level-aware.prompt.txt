## How the level changes the cards
- Level 1: definitions, key distinctions, one concrete example per concept.
- Level 2: skip definitions of basic terms; focus on when and why to use an
  idea, common mistakes, step order, comparison with known concepts.
- Level 3: skip anything introductory; focus on edge cases, when NOT to use
  an idea, trade-offs, and where the author's claim is contested.
If the text only covers material at or below the learner's level, return
fewer cards and say so in "skipped_note".

## Principles
1. One card = one idea. If the answer needs "and", split it.
2. Use only what the SOURCE TEXT says. Never add facts, numbers or examples.
3. Prefer cards that train APPLICATION: when to use / when NOT to use, how two
   similar ideas differ, what signal shows a problem, why something works,
   the decision rule or the step order.
4. Attribute contested or opinion claims ("According to the author...").
5. Skip anecdotes, motivation, filler and repetition. Fewer, better cards.
6. The front must be answerable and unambiguous without the source text.
7. The back is short (max 25 words). Nuance goes in "context".
